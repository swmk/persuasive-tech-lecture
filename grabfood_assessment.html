Primary Task SupportHelping the user get the core task — ordering food — done efficiently. 4 of 7 met.

Reduction
Simplify complex tasks by reducing steps or decisions.
Yes
Address selection is one tap on a saved address card rather than typing or searching. Counterpoint: the mandatory confirm-then-browse gate still adds a step at the flow level even as address entry itself is simplified.
Manual audit said No
Tunneling
Guide users through a predefined sequence toward a goal.
Yes
Navigation logic hard-gates the flow: address confirmation always precedes browsing, and any address change resets the stack back to the confirm screen before screen-home is reachable again.
Tailoring
Provide information or features relevant to the user's needs or context.
Yes
Restaurant list and promo copy are scoped to the confirmed address — "We'll only show restaurants, delivery fees, and delivery times for this address" — and the flash-sale title reads the current address name dynamically. Context-based adaptation, distinct from identity-based Personalization.
Manual audit said No
Personalization
Customize the system experience for individual users.
No
The three saved addresses (School, Home, Office) are static hardcoded data, not derived from user history or behavior. Nothing adapts to who the specific user is beyond location context — that's Tailoring, not Personalization.
Manual audit said Yes (location-based) — attributed to Tailoring instead
Self-monitoring
Allow users to track their own progress.
Yes
The min-spend widget shows explicit progress toward a goal — "S$8.60 more to unlock 25% off (S$3.40 of S$12.00)" — backed by a progress bar rendered at 28% width.
Simulation
Show cause-and-effect between actions and outcomes.
No
No feature previews an outcome (e.g. "if you add $X, here's your new total or ETA") — the progress bar reports current state only, not a simulated result.
Manual audit marked Yes but flagged "??" (self-identified as uncertain)
Rehearsal
Allow users to practice a target behavior before performing it for real.
No
No practice or sandbox mode exists; ordering food has no rehearsal analog in this prototype.
Manual audit marked Yes but flagged "??" (self-identified as uncertain)

Dialogue SupportFeedback and interaction quality between system and user. 3 of 7 met.

Praise
Provide positive feedback for desired actions.
No
No copy acknowledges a completed user action. The progress bar is a static 28% width with no wiring that fires a message when the spend threshold is reached — nothing dynamic exists in the current build.
Manual audit said Yes ("when amount reaches limit") — describes intended behavior not present in the static prototype
Rewards
Offer incentives or recognition for desired behaviors.
Yes
The flash banner offers a tangible incentive — 25% off — tied directly to completing an order, with per-item discounted prices shown against a struck-through original.
Reminders
Prompt users to continue or return to an activity.
No
The countdown is in-session urgency, not a prompt to return later — there's no push/notification scaffolding. More precisely an urgency mechanic supporting Suggestion.
Manual audit said Yes ("time count down") — attributed to Suggestion instead
Suggestion
Recommend actions at appropriate moments.
Yes
The min-spend copy nudges a specific next action — "S$8.60 more to unlock 25% off" — at the moment it's relevant, steering toward a larger cart.
Manual audit said No
Similarity
Reflect users' characteristics or preferences.
No
Interface doesn't mirror user traits or preferences — no adapted tone, avatar, or language matching the user.
Liking
Use an attractive, appealing interface to increase engagement.
Yes
Polished, modern visual design — gradient banners, rounded cards, consistent iconography, brand color — aimed at visual appeal. Inherently a perceptual/design-taste judgment.
Manual audit said No (marked "Subjective")
Social Role
Adopt the role of a coach, assistant, or advisor.
No
No persona (coach, assistant, advisor) is adopted; the UI is transactional, not conversational.

System Credibility SupportSignals of trustworthiness and competence. 3 of 7 met.

Trustworthiness
Present honest, unbiased, and reliable information.
Yes
Revised after discussion: the "⚠ Longer wait, restaurant is busy" note is the app volunteering negative information instead of hiding it to protect the sale — a genuine honesty signal.
Manual audit said Yes ("Longer wait message") — confirmed correct, original assessment revised from No
Expertise
Demonstrate knowledge and competence in the subject area.
No
Nothing demonstrates subject-matter competence — no curation rationale, food-safety info, or expert picks.
Surface Credibility
Maintain a professional, polished appearance.
Yes
Professional layout, consistent typography and spacing, realistic status bar and phone chrome give it a polished, credible look typical of a shipped app. Inherently a perceptual/design-taste judgment.
Manual audit said No (marked "Subjective")
Real-World Feel
Show information about the real people/organization behind it.
No
No info about the real company or people behind the app appears on these screens — no "About Grab," no support contact.
Authority
Reference recognized authorities or institutions.
No
No references to recognized institutions, certifications, or regulatory bodies.
Third-Party Endorsements
Display independent reviews, certifications, or endorsements.
Yes
Star ratings with review counts per restaurant — e.g. "★ 4.7 (122)" — function as crowd-sourced third-party endorsement signals.
Verifiability
Enable users to verify the accuracy of claims through external sources.
No
Ratings and claims aren't linkable or sourced — no way to click through to verify the "4.7 (122)" figure or read the underlying reviews. The rating is a claim, not a checkable one.
Manual audit said Yes ("Reviews") — the count itself isn't a verification mechanism without a drill-down

Social SupportLeveraging social influence and connection. 0 of 7 met.

Social Learning
Allow users to observe and learn from others' behaviors.
No
No feature exposes other users' visible choices or behavior — no "customers also ordered," no activity feed. A static review count is not observable behavior.
Manual audit said Yes ("Reviews") — closer to Third-Party Endorsement than Social Learning
Social Comparison
Enable users to compare their performance with others.
No
No comparison of the user's activity or spend against others.
Normative Influence
Encourage behavior by showing what others commonly do.
No
The "⚠ Longer wait, restaurant is busy" note signals operational status, not social norms ("most people order X") — no explicit norm-based messaging exists.
Social Facilitation
Increase motivation by showing others performing the same activity.
No
No indication of other users currently active or ordering alongside this user.
Manual audit said No (marked "Not live")
Cooperation
Encourage users to work together toward shared goals.
No
Single-user ordering flow; no shared or group goal mechanic.
Competition
Motivate users through friendly competition.
No
No leaderboard, streak, or competitive framing.
Recognition
Publicly acknowledge users' achievements or contributions.
No
No public acknowledgment of user achievements — no badges, no milestones.
